## Highlights

- **Renderer CPUs for Chromium and Electron apps.** `pvm app config <vm> --render-cpus N`, 2 by default, keeps the renderers of apps such as Claude Desktop on vCPUs 0 to N-1. In a 10-vCPU WHPX VM they drew visibly faster that way than on all of them. The browser process and what the app runs, such as Claude Code in Claude Desktop, keep every vCPU. `0` lifts the limit, and the setting applies to apps started afterwards. PVM Desktop has it as a slider in the app settings and the create form.
- **Input method text in one step.** Text from a host input method, such as Microsoft Pinyin, now goes into the focused app through an ibus engine that pvm adds to the VM's Xpra server. Before, it was typed with xdotool one character at a time. Full-width punctuation no longer turns into keys: `（` pressed BackSpace, `）` Tab, `；` Escape and `￥` Caps Lock.
- **Candidates at the text cursor.** On Windows hosts, the input method's candidate window opens at the app's text cursor in GTK apps, Firefox and Electron apps such as Claude Desktop. In Electron apps it follows a click, a view switch and a committed word too.
- **Less CPU for sound.** With nothing playing, the audio path woke the VM's vCPUs over a thousand times a second, and under WHPX every such wakeup exits to QEMU. Now:
  - The VM's sound is read every 50 ms and sent in 60 ms Opus frames, and the microphone comes in 60 ms frames.
  - Nothing is sent while the VM plays nothing. This is `pvm app config <vm> --audio-silence skip`, the default; `send` turns it off. PVM Desktop has it in the app settings and the create form.
  - With Claude Desktop open and nothing playing in a 10-vCPU WHPX VM, function-call IPIs fell from 1,500 to 1,900 a second to about 800. QEMU's share of a host core fell from 85-135% to 54-69%.
  - Sound reaches the host about 80 ms later, and the microphone reaches the VM about 40 ms later.
- **The microphone follows the default input device.** Plugging a headset in or out now restarts the microphone on the new default device. Before, the VM kept recording the old device, or heard nothing once it was gone, until the client was attached again.
- **Firefox fixes.** Its maximize button works again: the Windows client had removed it because of Firefox's oversized maximum window size. `pvm app install dev1 firefox` also installs over Ubuntu's snap placeholder package, which apt had refused as a downgrade.

## Upgrading

- **Use the Windows installer.** Candidate placement, microphone switching and the microphone's 60 ms frames are patches to the Xpra client it ships. `pvm upgrade` still replaces `pvm.exe` only.
- **Restart the VM's Xpra server.** The input method engine, the cursor tracking and the audio changes run in the Xpra server inside the VM, and take effect the next time it starts. Stop the VM's apps with `pvm app stop <vm> --all`, or restart the VM. A server started by an earlier version keeps the old behavior until then.
- **When the new settings apply.** `--render-cpus` applies to apps started afterwards, and `--audio-silence` to clients attached afterwards.

## Known issues

- In Chromium and Electron apps, a text cursor that moves without a click, a focus change or committed text, as when the text scrolls, is not reported. The candidates stay where they were until the next of those.
- On macOS and Linux hosts the candidates still open at the top left of the window, and the microphone does not follow a new default device.
- Programs without ibus still get input method text typed with xdotool.
- WHPX VMs still have no AVX. Exposing XSAVE with the bundled QEMU crashed a VM after about 38 minutes, so it stays off until a QEMU build syncs XSAVE correctly.
